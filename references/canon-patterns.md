# Canon and Long-Term Songcraft Patterns

Use this file to learn durable songwriting decisions from historically successful songs without copying their protected expression.

## Research sources

- [McGill Billboard Project](https://ddmal.ca/research/The_McGill_Billboard_Project_%28Chord_Analysis_Dataset%29/) provides CC0 chord, structure, metre, key and timing annotations for a large Billboard sample.
- [BiMMuDa](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.168) provides manually checked melodic and section data drawn from top year-end singles from 1950–2022.
- [Million Song Dataset](https://millionsongdataset.com/) provides large-scale audio features and metadata for historical comparison.
- [Lyrics segmentation research](https://aclanthology.org/C18-1174/) supports using repetition as evidence of macrostructure such as Verse and Chorus.
- [Song-corpus keyness research](https://aclanthology.org/2023.jlcl-1.3/) warns that raw word frequency is misleading when repeated Chorus words are concentrated in only a few songs.

Check each source's license before redistributing data. MusicBrainz core metadata is CC0, while supplementary data has different terms; see [MusicBrainz data licensing](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/About/Data_License).

## What to learn

### Lyrics

- Section function: what new image, fact, action or decision each section contributes.
- Hook mechanism: title placement, exact repetition, controlled mutation, call-and-response or melodic catchphrase.
- Viewpoint: who speaks, to whom, and where the relationship changes.
- Image system: recurring object, place, sensory detail and scale.
- Syntax and density: sentence length, clause count, conversational versus literary register.
- Sound pattern: stress, mora load, rhyme family, vowel echo, consonance and deliberate silence.
- Contrast: how Verse detail becomes Chorus compression and how Bridge reframes the premise.

### Composition and arrangement

- Form: section order, number of unique sections, repeat schedule and time to first Hook.
- Melody: range, phrase length, repetition, sequence, pickup, climax note and Verse-to-Chorus contour change.
- Harmony: tonal center, chord vocabulary, loop length, harmonic rhythm, cadence, borrowed chord and modulation.
- Rhythm: metre, subdivision, syncopation, phrase placement and density change between sections.
- Arrangement: entry and removal of instruments, register, texture, backing-vocal role and final-Chorus escalation.
- Production: energy curve and contrast. Do not treat loudness or density as a substitute for composition.

Chorus research finds that prominence involves multiple interpretable features, not repetition alone. Complexity research likewise suggests popular music clusters around an accessible range rather than proving that simpler is always better. See [chorus-feature analysis](https://ismir2013.ismir.net/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/180_Paper.pdf) and [music complexity study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04292).

## Balanced canon sampling

Build a private research sample across:

- decade rather than only recent memory;
- language and market rather than one chart;
- genre and tempo;
- solo, duet, group and instrumental-led work;
- immediate-Hook and slow-build forms;
- short and long recordings;
- major hits and influential outliers.

Do not promote a pattern from one famous song. Require recurrence across at least three unrelated works or label it `single-work observation`.

## Derive rules, not replicas

Allowed derived note:

> The Chorus reduces syntax, repeats one title phrase and raises the melodic register after a detail-heavy Verse.

Disallowed storage:

- complete or reconstructable lyrics;
- complete chord sheets or melodies tied to a protected song;
- distinctive phrase banks organized by artist;
- instructions to recreate a named recording;
- audio, stems or MIDI without redistribution rights.

Musical compositions and sound recordings are distinct protected works; a composition includes its music and lyrics. See [U.S. Copyright Office Circular 56A](https://copyright.gov/circs/circ56a.pdf). Apply stricter local or source-specific terms when relevant.

## Turn research into a Suno brief

1. Choose one structural principle, one lyric principle and one arrangement principle.
2. Express them without artist or song names.
3. Combine them with the user's own subject, Hook and image system.
4. Create an A/B test changing no more than two principles.
5. Keep the rule only if the generated result shows the intended difference.

## Validation questions

- Is the observation supported by multiple works or clearly labeled as a single case?
- Can the prompt be understood without knowing the source artist?
- Does the output use new wording, melody and story?
- Is the claimed effect observable in the generated result?
- Are source date, market, license and uncertainty recorded?
