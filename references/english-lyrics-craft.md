# English Lyrics Craft

Use this guide when the primary lyric language is English. Treat these as singability heuristics, not guarantees of a particular Suno rendering.

## Build the lyric in this order

1. State each section's job in one sentence.
2. Choose a compact Hook that can survive exact repetition.
3. Mark the naturally stressed syllables in the Hook and important lines.
4. Put content words and emotional pivots on strong or sustained positions where possible.
5. Draft for idiomatic speech before adding rhyme.
6. Add rhyme, internal echo and consonance without changing the intended register.
7. Read the lyric aloud at the target energy and repair forced stress or crowded phrasing.

English text setting commonly aligns linguistically prominent syllables with musically prominent beats. A mismatch can be used deliberately, but do not let it happen accidentally in the Hook. See [song-prosody research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3225926/) and [English lyric stress analysis](https://doi.org/10.15294/harmonia.v5i3.821).

## Stress and line shape

- Preserve the dictionary stress of multisyllabic words. Do not stretch an unstressed syllable merely to complete a rhyme.
- Let nouns, main verbs, adjectives and contrast words carry the strongest positions more often than articles, prepositions or auxiliary verbs.
- Keep neighboring Chorus lines similar in stress contour unless the contrast is intentional.
- Shorten the line before filling it with weak words. One clear action usually sings better than several clauses.
- For a long or high note, prefer a word whose stressed vowel can be sustained cleanly. Move dense consonant clusters away from the longest hold when possible.
- Use punctuation as phrasing guidance, not as decoration. Avoid prose-length sentences split arbitrarily across lyric lines.

## Rhyme without sounding manufactured

- Choose a rhyme family for the Hook, then mix perfect rhyme, slant rhyme, internal rhyme and unrhymed release lines.
- Do not reverse normal word order only to reach a rhyme.
- Avoid four consecutive lines that all end with the same grammatical construction unless repetition is the point.
- Judge rhyme by sound, not spelling. Account for the requested accent or dialect when pronunciation changes the match.
- Prefer semantic payoff over rhyme density: the final word should complete the thought, not merely complete the sound.

## Idiom, register and point of view

- Write as a speaker would naturally say it. Use contractions when the voice is conversational and full forms when distance, emphasis or formality is intended.
- Keep dialect, slang and spelling internally consistent. Do not mix several regional identities without a character reason.
- Avoid literal translations of Chinese four-character phrases, topic-comment structures or omitted subjects when the English result becomes unnatural.
- Keep pronouns and address stable. Make every shift from I to you, we or they narratively legible.
- Prefer specific actions and objects over unsupported abstractions. Replace generic declarations with evidence the listener can picture.

## Hook and section contrast

- Make the Hook understandable on first hearing and short enough to repeat without losing force.
- Place the title in or near the Chorus only when it strengthens recognition; do not force title placement into every section.
- Let Verse syntax carry more detail. Let Chorus syntax become simpler, more parallel or more repetitive.
- Give the Bridge a new fact, decision, time frame or point of view. Do not only restate the Chorus with synonyms.

## English mixed with Chinese or Japanese

- Assign a primary language to each section before drafting.
- Use English code-switches for a clear function: Hook, quoted speech, rhythmic lift or identity marker.
- Do not insert English solely because a rhyme is difficult in the primary language.
- Recheck the transition on both sides; the English phrase must be natural on its own and coherent in context.

## Revision audit

- Read every line aloud without melody. Repair words whose natural stress must be distorted.
- Underline the stressed vowel of every sustained Hook word.
- Remove filler articles, adverbs and explanatory clauses that do not change the image or action.
- Confirm that rhyme supports meaning and that repeated lines gain context on each return.
- Confirm that the lyric does not imitate a named artist's distinctive wording or reuse protected lyrics.
