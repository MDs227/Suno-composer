# Japanese Lyrics Craft

Use this guide when the primary lyric language is Japanese. Treat the rules as pronunciation and singability controls, not as a demand that every mora receive exactly one note.

## Build the lyric in this order

1. Fix the speaker, listener, relationship and politeness level.
2. Define each section's function and select one compact Hook.
3. Count morae in candidate Hook lines, including special morae.
4. Decide where the melody may compress, split or sustain morae.
5. Draft idiomatic Japanese with stable particles and sentence endings.
6. Add repetition, vowel echo and parallel syntax before forcing end rhyme.
7. Read the lyric aloud and check every kanji reading that could be ambiguous.

Japanese songwriting commonly treats the mora as a basic timing unit, while modern pop can flex the mapping between morae and notes. See [Japanese lyric-writing research](https://www.apsipa.org/proceedings_2012/papers/120.pdf) and [evidence for mora timing](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1362825893304415488).

## Mora and pronunciation

- Count ん, small っ and the second half of a long vowel as timing units. Treat contracted sounds such as きゃ as one mora.
- Do not assume one written character equals one beat or one note.
- Keep comparable Chorus lines reasonably similar in mora load, but allow a deliberate pickup, compression or melisma.
- Avoid placing a particle or weak grammatical fragment on the only long, high or emphatic note unless the effect is intentional.
- Prefer kana for a reading that Suno may mispronounce or when a kanji has several common readings. Do not romanize the whole lyric unless the user asks.
- Check word boundaries around ん, っ and vowel sequences so the sung result does not create an unintended word.

## Natural Japanese voice

- Choose plain, polite, literary or colloquial style and maintain it by character and section.
- Use subject omission only when the speaker and target remain clear. Restore a noun or pronoun when a Chorus would otherwise become ambiguous.
- Keep first-person forms, second-person address and sentence endings consistent with the established persona; never choose them from gender stereotypes alone.
- Use particles as meaning-bearing units. Do not drop は, が, を or に merely to satisfy a count if the result becomes unclear or unnatural.
- Prefer image plus action over stacked abstract nouns. Let emotion emerge from what the speaker notices, does or refuses to do.
- Avoid machine-translated noun piles and unnatural direct translations of English idioms.

## Sound pattern and rhyme

- Do not require English-style end rhyme in every pair of lines.
- Build cohesion through repeated key phrases, recurring vowels, parallel grammar, sound symbolism and controlled line endings.
- Use onomatopoeia only when it belongs to the speaker and scene; do not add it as a generic marker of Japanese style.
- Keep repeated Hook wording stable unless a final Chorus mutation carries a clear narrative payoff.

## Kanji, kana and visible layout

- Use normal Japanese orthography by default; switch a key word to katakana only for emphasis, foreignness, texture or brand-like impact.
- Avoid decorative kanji substitutions that obscure pronunciation.
- Keep line breaks aligned with syntactic or breath units. Do not split a compound or fixed phrase solely to make the page symmetrical.
- Add a parenthetical reading only when necessary and only if the target Suno workflow accepts it; otherwise rewrite with kana.

## English code-switching

- Give the English phrase one job: title Hook, quoted line, rhythmic accent or sectional contrast.
- Keep its vocabulary short and pronounceable in the surrounding tempo.
- Recheck the entry and exit particles. Do not attach Japanese grammar to an English phrase in a way the intended speaker would not use.
- If English dominates a full section, also read [english-lyrics-craft.md](english-lyrics-craft.md); do not load it for a single familiar loanword.

## Revision audit

- Count and compare mora load in repeated or parallel lines.
- Verify every ambiguous reading and every special mora.
- Confirm that particles, pronouns, politeness and sentence endings belong to one coherent persona.
- Remove literal translations, explanatory prose and decorative Japanese clichés.
- Confirm that repetition supports section recognition without reproducing a protected song or named artist's signature phrasing.
