# Current Trend Signals

Updated: 2026-08-17

Use this file only when the user asks for a contemporary direction. Treat it as a dated sampling layer, not a formula for quality. Refresh the linked charts before claiming that a signal is current.

## Evidence boundary

- Chart placement shows listening, sales or exposure under that chart's methodology; it does not prove artistic quality or causation.
- Title, artist, duration and chart persistence are metadata-level evidence. Do not infer exact lyric vocabulary, chords, tempo or instrumentation from them.
- Mark observations from one snapshot as `snapshot signal`; promote a rule only after it repeats across dates and markets.
- Never store full lyrics, chord sheets, MIDI or audio in this skill. Store only source links, metadata and derived patterns.

## Verified July 2026 snapshot signals

The accessible official snapshots were dated roughly 2026-07-20 to 2026-07-24. They are recent samples, not a verified 2026-08-17 ranking.

### Cross-market

- Current and catalog songs coexist near the top. A contemporary brief may borrow a durable structural principle, but must not equate `old` with `outdated` or copy a catalog work.
- Short one-idea titles coexist with longer conversational titles. Choose title length from the Hook's function rather than enforcing one universal limit.
- English titles and code-switching appear in Japan and Taiwan alongside local-language titles. Mixed language is a viable identity choice, not a requirement.
- Collaboration and soundtrack context are visible discovery routes. Use duet roles or scene framing when the user's story supports them, not as automatic trend decoration.

### Global / English snapshot

- Many visible titles use direct address, self-description, a concrete object or a compact emotional proposition.
- Lowercase styling and emphatic capitalization both appear; typography is branding metadata and should not replace a memorable phonetic Hook.
- Durations vary from very short tracks to long catalog recordings. Do not impose a single duration rule from the chart alone.

### Japan snapshot

- Japanese, English and bilingual titles coexist in the same top set.
- Compact noun titles, declarative phrases and heightened exclamatory wording all appear.
- Several catalog songs remain prominent, so test `immediate Hook` and `slow emotional build` as separate directions rather than assuming all J-pop must be short.

### Taiwan snapshot

- Mandarin relationship statements and image-pair titles coexist with English, Korean, Japanese and Taiwanese-language tracks.
- Film and drama associations remain visible in titles, making a clearly defined scene or narrative premise useful when genuine.
- Local catalog songs recur beside new releases. A trend brief should distinguish `new-release surface` from `durable sing-along memory`.

## Apply signals without cloning the chart

1. Select one market and one snapshot date.
2. Extract at most three independent variables: title behavior, section speed, language mix, vocal arrangement or emotional directness.
3. Keep the user's subject, images and viewpoint fixed.
4. Generate an A/B pair that changes only those variables.
5. Record what Suno actually produced; do not declare a trend useful until an observable result improves.

## Monthly refresh protocol

1. Capture the top 20 metadata rows for Global, Japan and Taiwan on the same date.
2. Record title, language, duration, release age if verifiable, collaboration and soundtrack flag.
3. Sample multiple weeks before calling any change a trend.
4. For lyric research, record only derived counts such as line length, repetition ratio, viewpoint and code-switch ratio; do not commit source lyrics.
5. For composition research, use licensed or open annotations and record section timing, metre, tonal center, harmonic rhythm and Chorus lift.
6. Date every conclusion and downgrade stale signals instead of silently carrying them forward.
